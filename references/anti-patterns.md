# Anti-patterns for Formative Design and Review

These patterns describe recurrent failure mechanisms, not universal visual
bans. Use only the entries relevant to the artifact and medium. A finding needs
observable evidence and a communication consequence; an AP identifier alone is
not a finding.

| ID | Failure mechanism and observable symptom | Counterexample | Formative question | Review evidence | Medium limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AP01 | Repeated cards or panels make evidence, examples, and conclusions look equally important. Symptom: the eye encounters a grid of equivalent boxes before the primary claim. | A comparison table uses repeated cells to make one variable comparable. | Which repeated units represent the same relationship, and which need hierarchy instead? | Rendered scan path, component repetition, and relative area. | Applies to fixed and fluid layouts; card count alone is not evidence. |
| AP02 | Decorative treatment is chosen before the information relationship. Symptom: color, icon, gradient, or boundary remains after its explanatory role is removed. | A color family distinguishes source, analysis, and inference. | What relationship does this treatment make faster to understand? | Element purpose mapped to nearby content. | Interaction affordances may require distinctions absent in print. |
| AP03 | A component or vendor aesthetic substitutes for a design decision. Symptom: the artifact resembles a system demo more than its communication task. | A design system supplies accessible components while content hierarchy remains local. | Which authority governs this problem, and what does it not decide? | Component choice tied to audience, task, and constraint. | Most acute in UI; can also affect slides, posters, and reports. |
| AP04 | Repeated emphasis competes with the primary message. Symptom: multiple headings, badges, calls to action, or figures demand first attention. | A dashboard legitimately has several monitored priorities with a stated order. | What must be seen first, and what can become supporting information? | Relative scale, position, contrast, and reading order in the render. | Sequential media can distribute emphasis over time. |
| AP05 | Visual hierarchy is too flat. Symptom: primary evidence, caveats, examples, and navigation have similar weight. | A reference table deliberately equalizes rows for comparison. | Which evidence should occupy the clearest and largest region? | Hierarchy map compared with rendered prominence. | Projection distance and physical viewing distance change the threshold. |
| AP06 | Copy names an abstract activity without its object, action, evidence, or limit. Symptom: headings such as "consideration" or "improvement" do not say what is being considered. | A short abstract heading is paired with a concrete subtitle and evidence. | Can a reader name the object, action, and reason without reading the full paragraph? | Text and nearby source or figure establish the missing terms. | Applies across media; short-contact media may require a tighter version. |
| AP07 | Data lacks a traceable basis. Symptom: a number, proportion, chart, or claim has no denominator, unit, version, derivation, or source path. | A compact figure caption gives dataset version, unit, and aggregation note. | Could another reader recompute or correctly bound this value? | Source record, calculation, denominator, unit, and version. | Applies to all media, including static posters and print. |
| AP08 | Precision is overstated through unvalidated scores, thresholds, or rankings. Symptom: arbitrary coverage percentages, item-count gates, or "AI-like" scores decide quality. | A validated domain threshold is named with its source and scope. | What decision does this number support, and is its threshold warranted? | Validation source, method, and uncertainty; otherwise descriptive framing only. | Applies wherever a metric influences a design or review decision. |
| AP09 | Meaning structure is lost during a derivative or transformation. Symptom: headings, labels, source/analysis distinctions, table relationships, or reading order disappear in an HTML, PDF, slide, or image version. | A derivative retains semantic hierarchy while adapting layout and density. | Which meaning relationships must survive this derivative? | Source-to-derivative comparison and semantic or structural inspection. | Applies to HTML-derived versions as well as exported files. |
| AP10 | A design is transferred between media by shrinking or copying rather than adapting. Symptom: a poster becomes unreadable slides or a responsive page preserves fixed-canvas assumptions. | Shared tokens and figures are re-composed for a new reading context. | What must remain semantically consistent, and what must change for this medium? | Target-medium render and comparison to the communication job. | Applies only when a derivative medium is in scope. |
| AP11 | Production is treated as proof of communication. Symptom: a build, export, or successful command is reported as if the audience experience had been verified. | A PDF export is followed by visual inspection at intended size. | What can only be learned from the rendered target artifact? | Render, playback, physical check, or explicit report that it is unavailable. | Use the verification relevant to the medium; not all artifacts need playback. |
| AP12 | Available space is automatically filled. Symptom: added text or graphics reduce pacing without improving a decision, explanation, or traceability. | A small evidence path clarifies a previously unsupported claim. | Is the best use of this space addition, clarification, visualization, or retention? | Before/after hierarchy and stated value, evidence, and reading cost. | Large print canvases and long pages need different pacing judgments. |
| AP13 | A local style preference becomes a universal prohibition. Symptom: cards, shadows, gradients, or other devices are banned regardless of role and medium. | A subtle surface separates an interactive control group; a gradient encodes a documented scale. | What harm occurs here, and could the device serve a necessary role? | Rendered role, accessibility, and communication impact. | Device suitability depends on medium, production, and accessibility constraints. |
| AP14 | Copy improvement erases the research or operational limit. Symptom: uncertainty, retrospective status, scope, or causal reservation disappears to make a claim sound decisive. | A concise claim keeps a nearby, legible qualification. | Which qualification changes the reader's warranted conclusion? | Source language, claim level, and placement of the caveat. | Applies especially to research communication, but also to policy and operational artifacts. |
| AP15 | Resources are listed without governing a decision. Symptom: design-system links, asset libraries, or authorities appear as an inventory rather than a justified route. | A named authority is tied to one implementation or communication decision and its scope. | What problem does this resource solve, and where does its authority end? | Resource-to-decision mapping, current terms when relevant. | Some media need no external resource beyond project or venue rules. |
| AP16 | Review criteria confuse implementation correctness with communication quality. Symptom: tests pass while hierarchy, evidence, or audience comprehension remains unexamined. | A report separates structural checks, rendered checks, and communication findings. | Which conclusion is about technical execution, and which is about the audience's understanding? | Distinct validation results and review findings. | All media need the distinction; the evidence type varies by medium. |

## Using the table

For formation, use an AP entry to ask a design question before committing space
or style. For review, apply it only after confirming that its symptoms appear in
the named artifact. When a pattern does not apply because the medium lacks the
relevant feature, mark it not applicable rather than manufacturing a finding.
